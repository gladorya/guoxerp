Authorization through Login Kit

Connecting an account opens TikTok's official authorization window in your browser. You sign in as yourself, review the requested permissions, and approve. The application receives a publishing token and nothing else: it never sees your TikTok password, your email, or your private messages.

The token refreshes automatically before it expires, so a connected account stays connected without you doing anything. You can revoke it at any time from your TikTok settings, and the application stops working with that account the moment you do.

Publishing: two modes

Direct post sends your video straight to your profile with the caption you wrote. TikTok processes it and it goes live on your page.

Draft sends the same video to your TikTok inbox instead. You review it in the TikTok app, adjust anything you want, and tap Post. This mode exists because TikTok requires it for applications still in review, and because many creators want the final say before anything goes live.

Both modes run through the same upload pipeline: the video transfers in chunks, TikTok processes it in the background, and the application tells you the moment it is done, with a link to the result.

Multiple accounts

Connect as many TikTok accounts as you manage. Each one keeps its own authorization, its own posting history, its own statistics and its own schedule. One account being rate limited by TikTok never affects another, because the application treats that as a signal to back off from that account only.

Switching between accounts is one click in the dashboard, and the account selector shows which ones are connected, which need attention, and why.

Captions written from the clip itself

When you publish a clip, the application transcribes its speech locally, translates it if the streamer was speaking another language, and turns it into a clean English title and caption that describe what happens in the moment. The original streamer is credited in every caption, and a note explains that clips are removed on request.

Profanity is filtered automatically. The text rules are enforced by the application itself: they are not a setting you can accidentally turn off, and they are not a request that a language model can choose to ignore.

Scheduling that respects your audience

Uploads keep a minimum gap between two posts on the same account, so your profile stays active across the day instead of posting five videos in ten minutes and going quiet. If a post comes due outside the hours your audience is actually online, it waits for the next window rather than going out at three in the morning.

And if TikTok itself tells the application to slow down, the application stops immediately and waits. Retrying a platform block is what makes blocks last, so the application treats that as a signal to back off, never as a challenge.

Statistics read live from TikTok

The dashboard shows your follower count, your video count, and the views and likes on each published video. Everything is read directly from TikTok every time the dashboard refreshes, so the numbers you see are the numbers TikTok has.

Uploads that TikTok is still processing are shown as processing, so you always know the difference between something that went out and something still in the pipeline.

Post management

Every published video is listed with its id and its stats. If a post should come down, you enter its id and confirm, and the application asks TikTok to remove it. A mistake published at midnight does not have to wait until morning.

Privacy by architecture

The application runs on your computer. There is no account on our side, no cloud sync, no analytics. Your tokens, your settings and your posting history live in the application's own folder on your machine, and the only network traffic is the direct API calls to TikTok that perform what you asked.
